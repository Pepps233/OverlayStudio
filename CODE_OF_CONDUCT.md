# Overlay Studio Code of Conduct

## Our Community

Overlay Studio brings together people creating banners, sharing artwork, reporting problems, and developing the editor. Everyone should be able to take part without harassment or discrimination, regardless of their background, identity, experience, or familiarity with software development.

This code applies to project issues, pull requests, reviews, and other project-managed spaces. It also applies when someone represents Overlay Studio in a public community setting.

## Expected Participation

- Be patient with questions and explain feedback in terms the other person can use. A first-time contributor or someone reporting an editor problem deserves the same respect as an experienced developer.
- Discuss the work rather than judging the person. Explain how a design or code change affects banner creation, accessibility, reliability, or maintenance.
- Make disagreements specific and constructive. Ask for clarification, acknowledge uncertainty, and be willing to revise your position when new information appears.
- Respect other people's time and boundaries. Keep discussions relevant and do not demand immediate responses, reviews, or releases.
- Respect privacy and creative ownership. Get permission before sharing another person's images or personal information, and credit artwork as its license requires.
- Be honest about what a contribution does, how it was tested, and any known limitations. Review expectations belong in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md); mistakes or lack of experience are not misconduct.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following are not welcome:

- Harassment, threats, discriminatory remarks, sexual harassment, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Personal insults, intimidation, targeted ridicule, or retaliation against someone who raises a concern.
- Sharing private information, private communications, or personal images without permission.
- Repeatedly derailing discussions, spamming project spaces, or continuing unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Knowingly submitting malicious code, concealing a security risk, or misrepresenting permission to distribute an asset.
- Pressuring others to bypass review or ignore safety and privacy concerns.

Good-faith bug reports, respectful disagreement, and constructive criticism of the project are welcome, even when they are uncomfortable to hear.

## Reporting Concerns

For conduct concerns, contact a repository maintainer privately using a contact method they make available on their GitHub profile. The current repository owner's profile is [Pepps233](https://github.com/Pepps233). This project does not currently list a dedicated conduct-reporting mailbox; do not assume that a public issue or PR is a confidential reporting channel.

If no private contact method is available, you may use GitHub's reporting and blocking tools for abuse on GitHub. Do not publish sensitive details simply to obtain a maintainer's attention. If a concern involves one maintainer, approach another uninvolved maintainer when one is available, or use GitHub's reporting tools.

When safe to do so, provide links, dates, a description of what happened, and relevant evidence. Share only the personal information needed to understand the concern. You do not need to confront the person involved before reporting.

## Maintainer Response

Maintainers may ask for clarification and review the context before acting. They should avoid participating in decisions where they have a conflict of interest and limit disclosure of a report to those needed to address it. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed, particularly when GitHub must be involved.

Depending on the seriousness and pattern of behavior, maintainers may request a correction, issue a warning, remove or hide content, restrict participation temporarily, or block participation. Serious threats or harassment may warrant immediate restrictions. The aim is to protect participants and restore a constructive environment, not to punish ordinary technical mistakes.

Anyone affected by a moderation decision may ask an uninvolved maintainer, where available, for reconsideration through a private contact method. Requests for reconsideration must not be used to continue unwanted contact with a reporter.
